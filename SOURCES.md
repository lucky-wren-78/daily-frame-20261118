# Sources — what each panel of entry.png is based on

from that day's reporting; every story is backed by at least two independent
sources. Numbering matches the numbered dots on the map, the numbered chips on

## 1 — APEC opens in Shenzhen as Xi, Trump and Putin circle a first trilateral (lead)
Xi Jinping keynoted the APEC CEO Summit on the morning of the 18th, defending
the WTO-centred multilateral trading system and promising lower tariffs and
wider market access; Vladimir Putin also spoke at the CEO Summit. Donald Trump
arrived from Manila and was greeted by Xi at the airport; at the evening
banquet Trump and Putin shook hands across Xi, and Xi exchanged a first
handshake with Japan's Sanae Takaichi since the Taiwan row. The first-ever
Trump–Xi–Putin three-way meeting, and the leaders' plenary, fall on Thursday
19 November — no summit decisions were announced on the 18th.
Sources: Reuters ("Trump's historic meeting with Xi, Putin headlines Asia
trip", 18 Nov) and Reuters takeaways from day 2 of Trump's Asia tour (18 Nov);
BusinessWorld / AFP ("China's Xi calls for free and open trade at APEC
summit", 18 Nov); Euronews (18 Nov); Seoul Economic Daily (18 Nov). Takaichi
handshake: The Japan Times (18 Nov); South China Morning Post (18 Nov);
Straits Times / Bloomberg (18 Nov).

## 2 — Iran widens the Gulf war to Kuwait and Bahrain
Iranian drones struck the US air base at Ali Al Salem in Kuwait in the early
hours of Wednesday — Tehran's first attack on Kuwait since September — in
retaliation for US jets striking an IRGC missile site on Qeshm Island on
17 November. Kuwait said its defences intercepted the drones, naming no
origin and reporting no casualties. Bahrain intercepted an Iranian attack for
the third time in a week, issuing two shelter warnings before an all-clear.
Sources: Reuters ("Iran attacks US bases in Gulf countries despite diplomatic
efforts", 18 Nov); Associated Press via Military.com (18 Nov, 10:22 EST);
Just Security, "Early Edition: November 18, 2026".

## 3 — Peltola flips Alaska, and the Senate goes 56–44
The Associated Press projected Democrat Mary Peltola the winner over
Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan in Alaska's Senate race — the last Senate
race of the 2026 midterms to be called — with 50.1% (144,302 votes) to 45.8%
(131,844), clearing the 50% threshold so no ranked-choice round was needed.
The result makes the next Senate 56–44 Democratic; Peltola becomes the first
Alaska Native in the US Senate. Sullivan had not conceded; certification is
targeted for 25 November. The AP call came late on Tuesday US time (03:27 UTC
18 Nov) and was reported widely on Wednesday.
Sources: Associated Press via The Sen Times (18 Nov); Alaska News (18 Nov,
independent call); KHSU / NPR (18 Nov).

## 4 — "Rain bomb" floods South Florida with 15.3 inches
Overnight rain dumped up to 15.3 inches in 24 hours on Broward County (13.7
inches near North Perry Airport), falling at up to 7 inches an hour around
Hollywood–Fort Lauderdale. Broward County and the city of Hollywood declared
emergencies on Wednesday morning; all Broward public schools closed, hundreds
of drivers were stranded, homes flooded, and part of a synagogue roof in
Hollywood collapsed with no injuries. About 2,900 customers lost power.
Sources: The Washington Post (18 Nov); PEOPLE (18 Nov); Florida Today live
blog (18 Nov).

## 5 — Jeffries nominated as first Black Speaker candidate
In a closed ballot on Wednesday morning, House Democrats chose Hakeem Jeffries
(New York) as their Speaker nominee for the 120th Congress by 251 votes to 11
of 262 ballots, putting him on course to become the first Black Speaker in US
history when the full House votes on 3 January 2027. Jeffries was unopposed;
seven of the eleven no votes came from new progressive members. Katherine
Clark becomes the first woman majority leader and Pete Aguilar the first
Latino whip.
Sources: Associated Press via Tulsa Daily Herald (18 Nov); CNN (18 Nov);
USA Today (18 Nov).

## 6 — EU proposes a €45bn loan for Ukraine in 2027
The European Commission proposed a €45 billion loan (about US$49.5bn) to
cover roughly two-thirds of Kyiv's €70bn funding gap in 2027: up to €20bn in
budget support and €25bn in military aid. The "limited recourse" loan would
only have to be repaid by Ukraine once Russia pays war reparations. It
complements the €90bn already agreed for 2026–2027; Commission President von
der Leyen said she expects all 27 member states to back it.
Sources: Reuters (18 Nov, 10:30 UTC); Agence Europe (18 Nov); Reuters
explainer (18 Nov).

## 7 — Brazil's Senate passes Lula's R$60bn fiscal package
Brazil's Senate approved President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva's fiscal
adjustment package by 69 votes to 2 on Wednesday, after a one-day delay for
lack of support. The package — spending curbs plus higher revenues worth up to
R$60 billion (US$10.29bn) in 2027, including a freeze on extra budgets if
needed and a cut in the annual real spending-growth cap from 2.5% to 2% — now
goes to the lower house. Finance Minister Dario Durigan called it key to a
0.5%-of-GDP primary surplus target next year.
Sources: Reuters (18 Nov, 22:33 UTC); katavNews (18 Nov); InfoMoney /
SpaceMoney (18 Nov).

## 8 — Bangladesh deploys paramilitary as Hasina's return looms
Bangladesh deployed hundreds of paramilitary personnel nationwide on
Wednesday after supporters of former PM Sheikh Hasina — who plans to return
from exile in India in December — set off clashes, crude-bomb blasts and
vehicle burnings in at least 28 districts over ten days. A bus helper was
burned to death in Pirojpur on Wednesday morning after two buses were torched;
37 Grameen Bank branches have been targeted since 10 November; Dhaka police
arrested at least 71 members of banned groups in 24 hours. The Awami League
called a nationwide lockdown for 19 November.
Sources: Reuters (18 Nov, 10:53 UTC); The Asia Times (18 Nov); Border Guard
Bangladesh release via The Library Estate (18 Nov).

## 9 — Ethiopia and Eritrea mass troops on their border
Ethiopia and Eritrea have deployed forces along their shared border, leaving
both armies within striking distance of each other, according to conflict
monitor ACLED and the International Crisis Group and sources close to Tigrayan
militias — raising fears of open war between the historic rivals. ACLED said
artillery fire and clashes occurred near Badme in the past week; an Ethiopian
drone struck a suspected Eritrean position on 12 November and the armies
clashed on 13 November. Reuters said it could not independently confirm the
clash reports; Eritrea's information minister called them propaganda.
Sources: Reuters (18 Nov); OkayAfrica (18 Nov, summarising the Reuters
report and the ACLED statement). The clashes themselves are reported, not
independently confirmed — the image says "reported".

## 10 — Malaysia's economy grows 5.5% on the AI hardware boom
Malaysia's Department of Statistics and Bank Negara announced on 18 November
that third-quarter GDP grew 5.5% year on year — above the 5.3% advance
estimate and consensus, though slower than 6.0% in Q2. Exports rose 25.2%,
with electrical and electronics exports up 66.9% on global demand for AI
hardware. Nine-month growth reached 5.6%; full-year 2026 growth is now
expected above 5%.
Sources: Ministry of Finance Malaysia press release (18 Nov); KLSE Screener
(18 Nov); The Leaders Online (18 Nov).

## 11 — Morocco to build 40% of its defence needs by 2035
King Mohammed VI chaired a working session at the Royal Palace in Rabat on
Wednesday to launch the "Morocco Offer", a national defence and security
industry roadmap. The Hassan II Fund contributes 4 billion dirhams (US$400
million); the 2035 targets are about 50,000 direct and indirect jobs, about
40% of the Royal Armed Forces' and security services' needs met locally, and
over 65% of sector revenue from exports — prioritising drones, land and sea
systems, defence electronics, cyber and AI. Two new defence industrial zones
are planned in Benguerir and Benslimane.
Sources: Reuters (18 Nov); Walaw Press (18 Nov); Global Arab Network
(18 Nov).

## 12 — Anthropic prices its IPO at $83 in an $81.3bn listing
Anthropic priced its IPO at US$83 a share on Wednesday, above the US$70–78

Sources: Reuters via WIXX (18 Nov); The Motley Fool (18 Nov); FinanceFeeds
(18 Nov).

## 14 — Voyager 1 becomes the first object one light-day from Earth

## 15 — Pope Leo XIV leads St Peter's 400th-anniversary Mass
Pope Leo XIV presided at the 400th-anniversary Mass in St Peter's Basilica at
17:00 Rome time on 18 November 2026, exactly 400 years after Pope Urban VIII
dedicated the basilica on 18 November 1626, ending with an apostolic blessing
at about 18:40. In his homily he said: "War will not prevail as long as we
proclaim peace. Poverty will not prevail as long as we witness to love."
It was his first Mass after returning from his 6–17 November apostolic trip
to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru.
Sources: National Catholic Register / CNA (18 Nov); Associated Press via
EuropeSays (18 Nov, 19:56 UTC); Vatican News via CathNews (18 Nov, 19:20
UTC); Holy See Press Office homily text B0931.

## 25 — Alberta curbs giant data centres; drops oil sands cap
Alberta announced legislation on Wednesday: data centres of 10 megawatts or
more may not be built within 600 metres of the nearest home, on the best
farmland, or on irrigated land, with new provincial water-use standards to
curb data-centre consumption. In the same package Alberta will scrap the
100-million-tonne oil sands emissions cap (in force since 2016, never
enforced; 2023 emissions were about 84 million tonnes), arguing the
industrial carbon price already gives enough incentive. (The cap detail rests
on Reuters; the government release confirms the data-centre limits.)
Sources: Reuters (Calgary, 18 Nov, 21:33 UTC); Government of Alberta news
release (18 Nov).

## 29 — EU clears signing of its Indonesia trade deal
The EU Council authorised on Wednesday the signature and provisional
application of two agreements with Indonesia: the Comprehensive Economic
Partnership Agreement (CEPA) and the Investment Protection Agreement (IPA).
Once fully in force, up to 98.5% of tariff lines are liberalised and 96% of
Indonesia's duties are removed within five years; Indonesia's 50% duty on EU
cars is also removed within five years. Formal signing is scheduled for
21 November, then the European Parliament. The deal was fast-tracked because
Indonesia graduates from the EU's GSP preference scheme on 1 January 2027.
Sources: Reuters (18 Nov, 18:28 UTC); Agence Europe (18 Nov); Government of
Ireland (gov.ie, 18 Nov, 16:09 UTC).

## 30 — Newsom: California must be able to switch off rogue AI
California Governor Gavin Newsom released recommendations on Wednesday in the
85-page report "AI Safety in 2026 and Beyond": the state should be able to
order an emergency shutdown of an AI model that poses catastrophic danger and
whose maker refuses to act, set mandatory safety standards for big frontier
labs, require ongoing independent inspections, and cut the deadline for
reporting serious incidents from 15 days to 48 hours. The recommendations are
not yet law — legislation is needed when the legislature returns in December.
Sources: New York Post (18 Nov); Unite.ai (18 Nov); Daily Caller (18 Nov).
